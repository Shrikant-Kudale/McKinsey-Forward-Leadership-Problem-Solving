# Executive Playbook: MECE Principle & Logic Trees

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **MECE and logic trees** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
MECE means branches are mutually exclusive (no overlap) and collectively exhaustive (no gaps). Diagnostic trees ask why, while lever trees ask how. Quantitative trees can be validated arithmetically; qualitative trees need a single organising principle at each level.

## Procedure
### Step 1
Choose a tree type: diagnostic (why) or lever (how).

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
Pick one organising principle per level, such as a formula, process stages or segments.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Draw branches and test exclusivity: could an item sit in two boxes?

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Test exhaustiveness: what is missing, and would an Other bucket exceed 10 percent?

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Validate numbers and prune branches that will not change the decision.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Mixing criteria such as segment and channel in one level
- A catch-all Other bucket that hides a major driver

## Worked example
**Ecommerce profit decline decomposed into a driver tree.** A profit decline was decomposed as Revenue minus Cost, with Revenue as customers times orders times average order value. The first version of the cost branch omitted G&A, leaving 2.8M unexplained; the checker flagged the gap and the corrected tree reconciled to the reported profit.

## Quality bar
- Each level uses one organising principle
- No item can sit under two branches
- Numbers reconcile to the stated total
- Branches map to analyses I can run
