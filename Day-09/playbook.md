# Executive Playbook: Hypothesis-Driven Problem Solving & 2x2 Prioritization

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **hypothesis-driven problem solving** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
Hypothesis-driven work starts from an answer-first guess (Day-1 hypothesis) and designs the fastest analyses to prove or disprove it. Impact vs. feasibility 2x2 matrices and weighted decision matrices keep prioritisation transparent.

## Procedure
### Step 1
Write a Day-1 hypothesis in a testable if-then-because sentence.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
List the evidence that would confirm or refute it.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Score candidate initiatives on weighted criteria.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Plot impact against feasibility on a 2x2 grid.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Stress-test the ranking with equal weights and decide.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Anchoring on a favourite and reverse-engineering weights
- Treating a hypothesis as a conclusion

## Worked example
**Retailer ranks five growth initiatives.** A retailer scored five initiatives on impact, feasibility, speed and fit. The weighted ranking placed the loyalty app relaunch first, but an equal-weights check promoted store-pickup lockers, prompting the team to fund lockers as a quick win and the app as the strategic bet.

## Quality bar
- My hypothesis is falsifiable
- Weights were agreed before scoring
- I ran an equal-weights sensitivity
- The 2x2 matches the ranked list
