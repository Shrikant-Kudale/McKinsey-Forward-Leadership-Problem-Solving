# Day 09 (2026-08-09): Hypothesis-Driven Problem Solving & 2x2 Prioritization

**McKinsey Forward alignment:** Session 3 - Structuring the Problem

## Framework breakdown
Hypothesis-driven work starts from an answer-first guess (Day-1 hypothesis) and designs the fastest analyses to prove or disprove it. Impact vs. feasibility 2x2 matrices and weighted decision matrices keep prioritisation transparent.

## Step-by-step methodology
1. Write a Day-1 hypothesis in a testable if-then-because sentence.
2. List the evidence that would confirm or refute it.
3. Score candidate initiatives on weighted criteria.
4. Plot impact against feasibility on a 2x2 grid.
5. Stress-test the ranking with equal weights and decide.

## Real-world case study: Retailer ranks five growth initiatives
A retailer scored five initiatives on impact, feasibility, speed and fit. The weighted ranking placed the loyalty app relaunch first, but an equal-weights check promoted store-pickup lockers, prompting the team to fund lockers as a quick win and the app as the strategic bet.

## Common pitfalls
- Anchoring on a favourite and reverse-engineering weights
- Treating a hypothesis as a conclusion
