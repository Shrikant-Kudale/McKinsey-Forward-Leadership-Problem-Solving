# Day 13 (2026-08-13): The Minto Pyramid Principle

**McKinsey Forward alignment:** Session 5 - Communicating for Impact

## Framework breakdown
The Pyramid Principle leads with the answer, supports it with three or fewer key arguments, and groups evidence either deductively (a logical chain) or inductively (parallel reasons). The governing thought is the single sentence the reader should remember.

## Step-by-step methodology
1. Write the governing thought: answer plus so-what.
2. List three or fewer supporting arguments.
3. Choose deductive or inductive grouping per argument.
4. Check that each level answers why or how of the level above.
5. Rewrite the opening paragraph so the answer is the first sentence.

## Real-world case study: Board memo on a supply-chain investment
A draft memo opened with three pages of background. Rewritten with the answer first (invest 6M in forecasting to cut stock-outs 30 percent) and three supports (value, feasibility, risk), the board approved in a single meeting instead of two.

## Common pitfalls
- Burying the answer after the context
- Supports that overlap
