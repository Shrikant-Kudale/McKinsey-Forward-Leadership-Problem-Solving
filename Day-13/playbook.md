# Executive Playbook: The Minto Pyramid Principle

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **Minto Pyramid Principle** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
The Pyramid Principle leads with the answer, supports it with three or fewer key arguments, and groups evidence either deductively (a logical chain) or inductively (parallel reasons). The governing thought is the single sentence the reader should remember.

## Procedure
### Step 1
Write the governing thought: answer plus so-what.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
List three or fewer supporting arguments.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Choose deductive or inductive grouping per argument.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Check that each level answers why or how of the level above.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Rewrite the opening paragraph so the answer is the first sentence.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Burying the answer after the context
- Supports that overlap

## Worked example
**Board memo on a supply-chain investment.** A draft memo opened with three pages of background. Rewritten with the answer first (invest 6M in forecasting to cut stock-outs 30 percent) and three supports (value, feasibility, risk), the board approved in a single meeting instead of two.

## Quality bar
- The answer is in the first sentence
- I have three or fewer supports
- Each level answers why or how
- The reader can stop after paragraph one and still decide
