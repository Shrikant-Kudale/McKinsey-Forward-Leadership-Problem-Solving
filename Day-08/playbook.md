# Executive Playbook: Quantitative Profitability & Driver Trees

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **profitability and driver trees** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
Profit equals revenue minus costs. Revenue decomposes into price, volume and mix; cost into fixed and variable. Driver trees expose which lever moves profit most per unit of effort, and a sensitivity run ranks them.

## Procedure
### Step 1
Decompose profit into revenue and cost drivers.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
Split revenue by segment into price x volume and note mix.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Split cost into variable per unit and fixed.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Apply a uniform 5 percent move to each lever and rank the impact.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Compute break-even volume and decide which lever to pursue first.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Comparing levers with unequal effort assumptions
- Ignoring mix when segment margins differ

## Worked example
**Hardware maker with three tiers and thin margin.** The company earned 0.56M profit on 8.8M revenue. Sensitivity showed a 5 percent variable-cost reduction was worth about 0.29M more than a 5 percent volume increase, so procurement became the first workstream rather than sales incentives.

## Quality bar
- My driver tree reconciles to reported profit
- I ranked levers on equal footing
- I know my break-even volume
- I chose a first lever with a rationale
