# Executive Playbook: Deconstructing Ambiguous Business Challenges

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **problem deconstruction and SCQ** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
A well-framed problem separates symptoms (what we observe) from root causes (why it happens). The Situation-Complication-Question framing anchors the context everyone agrees on, the change that creates tension, and the single question the work must answer.

## Procedure
### Step 1
List observed symptoms and tag each as fact or assumption.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
Write the Situation as uncontested context.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Write the Complication: what changed or broke.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Write the Key Question in one sentence that a decision-maker could answer.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Complete the Problem Statement Worksheet and test it with the sponsor.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Writing the solution inside the question
- A Situation that contains opinions

## Worked example
**Online marketplace sees repeat purchases decline.** The CEO asked how to boost marketing spend because repeat purchases fell. SCQ framing showed the situation (growth through acquisition), the complication (repeat rate fell from 34 to 27 percent in two quarters) and a different question: why are first-time buyers not coming back? Marketing spend was a premature solution.

## Quality bar
- I separated symptoms from causes
- My Situation statement is uncontested
- My Key Question is answerable with a decision
- The sponsor confirmed the framing
