# Executive Playbook: Psychological Safety & Team Norms

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **psychological safety** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
Google's Project Aristotle found psychological safety to be the strongest predictor of team effectiveness. Teams build it with working agreements, blameless post-mortems, and leaders who model fallibility and curiosity.

## Procedure
### Step 1
Baseline the team on a short psychological safety survey.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
Draft working agreements for meetings, feedback and decisions.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Adopt a blameless post-mortem format.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Model vulnerability: share one mistake and what you learned.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Re-survey after six weeks and compare.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Calling it blameless while still hunting for a culprit
- Norms written once and never revisited

## Worked example
**Engineering team recovers after a production incident.** After an outage the team ran a blameless post-mortem that focused on system conditions. Reported willingness to raise concerns rose in the next survey and near-miss reports tripled, which prevented two later incidents.

## Quality bar
- We have written working agreements
- Post-mortems focus on systems, not people
- I shared a mistake with the team
- We re-survey on a schedule
