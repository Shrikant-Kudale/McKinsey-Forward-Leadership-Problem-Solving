# Executive Playbook: Stakeholder Mapping & Power-Interest Grids

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **stakeholder mapping** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
Stakeholders differ in power (ability to affect the outcome) and interest (how much the outcome affects them). The power-interest grid, combined with stance from champion to blocker, gives each stakeholder a tailored engagement approach.

## Procedure
### Step 1
List every stakeholder and the decision they influence.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
Score power and interest from 1 to 10.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
Score stance from -2 (blocker) to +2 (champion).

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Assign an engagement strategy by quadrant.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Plan the first conversation with each high-power blocker.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Ignoring quiet high-power stakeholders
- Mapping once and never updating

## Worked example
**Data-platform migration across engineering and business teams.** A migration affected nine groups. Mapping showed the Security Lead had high power and a mild blocking stance because of access-control concerns; an early design review addressed them. Sales Ops, with low power but high interest, were kept informed and became vocal supporters.

## Quality bar
- I scored power, interest and stance
- High-power blockers have a plan
- Champions have a specific ask
- I will refresh the map monthly
