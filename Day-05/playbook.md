# Executive Playbook: SMART Problem Statements & Scope Boundary Engineering

## Purpose
Give a repeatable, 60-minute routine for **SMART problem statements and scope** that you can run on any project.

## Core idea
A strong problem statement names the decision-maker, success criteria, constraints, time horizon and scope boundaries. Explicit boundaries (in scope, out of scope, assumptions) prevent premature solutioning and scope creep.

## Procedure
### Step 1
Name the decision-maker and the decision they must make.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 2
Define success with a measurable target and a deadline.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 3
List constraints: budget, data, regulation, people.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 4
Draw scope boundaries: in, out and parked.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

### Step 5
Review the statement for solution bias and rewrite if needed.

**Deliverable:** one written paragraph or table that proves this step is complete.

## Watch-outs
- Vague success criteria such as improve retention
- Scope that silently expands every week

## Worked example
**SaaS company with rising monthly churn.** Churn rose from 2.1 to 3.4 percent monthly. The first draft statement was reduce churn by launching a loyalty program, which embedded a solution. The rewritten statement read: by 31 December, determine the primary drivers of SMB churn and recommend actions that bring monthly churn below 2.5 percent within a 1.5M budget.

## Quality bar
- The decision-maker is named
- Success is measurable and dated
- Constraints and scope boundaries are written
- No solution appears in the statement
