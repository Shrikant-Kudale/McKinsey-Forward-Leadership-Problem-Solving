# Day 05 (2026-08-05): SMART Problem Statements & Scope Boundary Engineering

**McKinsey Forward alignment:** Session 2 - Defining the Problem

## Framework breakdown
A strong problem statement names the decision-maker, success criteria, constraints, time horizon and scope boundaries. Explicit boundaries (in scope, out of scope, assumptions) prevent premature solutioning and scope creep.

## Step-by-step methodology
1. Name the decision-maker and the decision they must make.
2. Define success with a measurable target and a deadline.
3. List constraints: budget, data, regulation, people.
4. Draw scope boundaries: in, out and parked.
5. Review the statement for solution bias and rewrite if needed.

## Real-world case study: SaaS company with rising monthly churn
Churn rose from 2.1 to 3.4 percent monthly. The first draft statement was reduce churn by launching a loyalty program, which embedded a solution. The rewritten statement read: by 31 December, determine the primary drivers of SMB churn and recommend actions that bring monthly churn below 2.5 percent within a 1.5M budget.

## Common pitfalls
- Vague success criteria such as improve retention
- Scope that silently expands every week
